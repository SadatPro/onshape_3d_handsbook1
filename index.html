Chapter 0: Before You Begin

Who Should Take This Course?

This handbook is engineered for absolute beginners and transitioning professionals alike. Whether you are a student exploring mechanical engineering, a hobbyist designing custom parts for 3D printing, or a maker building drones and robots, this curriculum provides a structured, hands-on path to CAD mastery.

Hardware & Software Requirements
  • A standard computer/laptop: Works flawlessly on Windows, Mac, Linux, or even Chromebooks.
  • A 3-Button Mouse: A mouse with a clickable scroll wheel is mandatory for CAD navigation (rotating and panning). Trackpads will severely limit your workflow.
  • Internet Connection: Onshape is 100% cloud-based. There is nothing to download or install.

Why Onshape?

Unlike traditional legacy CAD software (such as SolidWorks or Inventor), Onshape runs entirely in your web browser. It leverages secure cloud computing, meaning it won't slow down your local machine. It offers built-in version control (like Google Docs for CAD), seamless collaboration features, and industry-standard parametric modeling tools.

1.3 Units and Document Settings

Always configure your document units before starting. For mechanical engineering, millimeters (mm) are standard.

Step1
Open a new document in Onshape
  • Go to onshape.com and log in
  • Click '+ New' > 'Document'
Step2
Set units
  • Click the 'Units' menu in the bottom-right corner
  • Select Millimeter, Gram, Second (MGS)
Step3
Enable grid
  • Turn on grid display for visual reference while sketching

Chapter 2: Sketching Fundamentals

Every 3D model begins as a 2D sketch. A sketch is a collection of lines, arcs, and curves drawn on a plane that is later turned into 3D geometry using features like Extrude or Revolve.

2.1 Sketch Planes

  • Front Plane (XY): Best for viewing parts from the front (default elevation)
  • Top Plane (XZ): Best for floor plans, top-down layouts
  • Right Plane (YZ): Best for side profiles

2.2 Core Sketch Tools

ToolPurpose
LineDraw straight edges and boundaries
Rectangle (Corner/Center)Create rectangular profiles quickly
Circle (Center/3-Point)Define circular cross-sections
Arc (3 types)Create partial circular edges
Polygon (N-sides)Regular polygon shapes
SlotElongated holes and slots
SplineOrganic/curved profiles
Construction GeometryNon-solid guide geometry (toggle with Q)

2.3 Sketch Constraints

Constraints define geometric relationships. A fully constrained sketch turns black; an underconstrained sketch shows blue geometry.

  • Coincident: Two points share the same location
  • Horizontal/Vertical: Forces a line to be perfectly H or V
  • Parallel: Two lines run in the same direction
  • Perpendicular: Two lines meet at 90 degrees
  • Tangent: A line or arc flows smoothly into another curve
  • Equal: Two elements share the same size/length
  • Symmetric: Elements mirror about a centerline
Pro TipAlways fully constrain your sketches before adding 3D features. A fully-defined sketch ensures your model updates predictably when you change dimensions.

Chapter 3: Core 3D Features

3.1 Extrude

Extrude pushes a 2D sketch profile along a straight axis to create a 3D solid or cut.

  • New: Creates a new solid body
  • Add: Merges with existing geometry (boolean union)
  • Remove: Cuts from existing geometry (boolean subtract)
  • Blind: Extrude a specific distance
  • Symmetric: Extrude equally in both directions
  • To Face/Vertex: Extrude up to a reference surface

3.2 Revolve

Revolve rotates a 2D profile around a centerline to create cylindrical and axially-symmetric shapes such as shafts, bottles, and wheels.

  • Select a closed profile sketch
  • Choose the axis of revolution (a line or axis)
  • Enter the angle of rotation (360° for a full revolution)

3.3 Fillet & Chamfer

FeatureDescription
FilletRounds an edge with a circular arc — adds material smoothness
ChamferCuts a flat angled face at an edge (often 45°)
ShellHollows out a solid to a uniform wall thickness
MirrorReflects one or more features across a plane
Circular PatternRepeats features around an axis (e.g., bolt holes on a flange)

Chapter 5: Design Best Practices

5.1 Parametric Modeling Habits

  • Always fully constrain sketches before creating features
  • Use variables (Onshape Variables feature) for dimensions you expect to change
  • Name all features in the Feature Tree — avoid 'Extrude 7' as a name
  • Build features in logical order: base shape first, refinements last

5.2 File and Document Management

  • Use version control: create a version before major edits (click the clock icon)
  • Export to STEP (.stp) for sharing with other CAD software, STL for 3D printing
  • Share documents with collaborators using the 'Share' button — Onshape is cloud-native

5.3 Design for Manufacturing (DFM)

ProcessKey Rules
3D Printing (FDM)Min wall 1.2mm, max overhang 45°, draft not required
CNC MachiningNo blind holes deeper than 3× diameter, prefer round internal corners
Sheet MetalBend radius = material thickness, k-factor 0.4–0.5
Injection MoldingDraft angle 1–3°, uniform wall thickness <4mm

Chapter 7: 1-Month Learning Roadmap

To effectively master 3D design without feeling overwhelmed, we recommend spreading the 10 models over a 4-week period. Consistency and hands-on practice are key to developing mechanical intuition.

Week1
The Fundamentals (approx. 5 Hours)

Focus on mastering the UI, basic sketching, constraints, and simple extrusions.

  • Read: Chapters 0, 1 & 2
  • Build: Model 1 (3D Vase) & Model 2 (Door Hinge)
  • Goal: Get comfortable navigating the 3D viewport and making sure your 2D sketches turn completely black (fully constrained).
Week2
Intermediate Operations (approx. 7 Hours)

Introduce hollow structures, cosmetic/helical threads, sweeps, and basic mechanical assemblies.

  • Build: Model 3 (iPhone Case) & Model 4 (Nut & Bolt)
  • Goal: Master the Shell tool for thin-walled parts, understand thread generation, and successfully mate two parts together using a Cylindrical mate.
Week3
Complex Geometry & Patterns (approx. 8 Hours)

Tackle aerodynamic shapes, lofts across offset planes, mirroring, and large-scale circular patterns.

  • Build: Model 5 (Turbine Blade), Model 6 (Wind Turbine), & Model 7 (Drone Frame)
  • Goal: Learn to use the Loft tool to blend profiles, and drastically reduce modeling time by utilizing the Mirror and Pattern tools.
Week4
Advanced Assemblies & Kinematics (approx. 10 Hours)

Combine multi-part modeling with complex rigging, robotic design, and realistic motion constraints.

  • Build: Model 8 (Starship), Model 9 (Robotic Arm), & Model 10 (Spot Robot)
  • Goal: Build multi-DOF (Degree of Freedom) assemblies, establish functional kinematic chains, and assign joint limits to prevent part collision.
